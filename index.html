Discover Fungi

A Field Guide to the Forest's Hidden Kingdom

From the delicate gills of Marasmius to the robust pores of Boletus, every species tells a story about the forest's health. Our regional guides help you identify, understand, and ethically engage with the fungi beneath your feet.

Seasonal Species Spotlight

Each season reveals different fungal citizens. Spring brings morels and oysters. Summer hosts chanterelles and chicken of the woods. Autumn is the kingdom of boletes, amanitas, and honey mushrooms. Even winter offers velvet foot and late oysters on thaw days.

  • Morchella spp. — True morels, spring indicators of healthy forest soils
  • Cantharellus cibarius — Golden chanterelles, mycorrhizal partners of oak and pine
  • Boletus edulis — Porcini, the king of boletes, abundant in mixed coniferous woods
  • Grifola frondosa — Hen of the woods, found at oak bases in autumn
  • Hericium erinaceus — Lion's mane, cascading spines on dead hardwood
  • Pleurotus ostreatus — Oyster mushrooms, year-round decomposers on hardwood

Identification Fundamentals

Safe foraging begins with careful observation. We teach a systematic approach: substrate and habitat, macroscopic features (cap, gills, pores, stem, veil), spore print color, bruising reactions, odor, and microscopic confirmation when needed. Never consume a mushroom you cannot identify with 100% certainty.

  • Document the substrate — soil, wood, dung, living tree
  • Note the season and recent weather patterns
  • Photograph cap, gills/pores, stem base, and cross-section
  • Take a spore print on white and black paper
  • Check for color changes on bruising or cutting
  • Consult multiple field guides and local experts

Our regional chapter leads offer hands-on identification walks throughout the foraging season.

Forest Stewardship

Reciprocity with the Land

Ethical foraging is not taking — it is participating in an ancient relationship. We harvest with gratitude, take only what is abundant, leave the substrate intact, and give back through habitat restoration and citizen science.

Ethical Harvest Protocols

Cut, don't pull. Leave the mycelium undisturbed. Take less than 10% of a patch. Use mesh bags to spread spores as you walk. Never harvest rare or red-listed species.

Citizen Science & Documentation

Every foray contributes to our regional species atlases. Members record observations via iNaturalist and our internal database, tracking phenology, distribution shifts, and habitat associations over time.

Habitat Restoration

We partner with land trusts and state parks to remove invasive species, inoculate logs with native saprotrophs, and monitor mycorrhizal recovery in disturbed sites. Members volunteer for seasonal workdays.

Land Access Agreements

Our chapter leads negotiate stewardship agreements with private landowners and public agencies, securing ethical foraging access in exchange for ecological monitoring and invasive species management.

Community & Chapters

You Need Fungi. Fungi Need You.

The soil of old-growth forests holds the key to planetary health. Its fungi regulate carbon, feed the trees, filter water, and heal damaged land. QuantumAggForage unites wild harvesters to protect these habitats and share knowledge across our regional chapters.

Regional Chapters

Each chapter operates semi-autonomously, adapted to its bioregion's fungal calendar, land access patterns, and conservation priorities. Chapter leads coordinate forays, manage land agreements, and mentor members.

  • NJ — Pine Barrens Chapter: Pitch pine–oak forests, Atlantic white cedar swamps, coastal plain ponds
  • PA — Appalachian Chapter: Mixed mesophytic forests, ridgetop acidic oak, limestone valley woods
  • NY — Catskills/Adirondacks Chapter: Northern hardwoods, spruce-fir zones, boreal outliers
  • FL — Sandhills Chapter: Longleaf pine savannas, scrub oak, sand pine scrub, wet flatwoods

Ways to Participate

  • Forager Member: Attend walks, access species guides, join Discord community, receive seasonal newsletter
  • Stewardship Volunteer: Habitat restoration workdays, invasive removal, mycorrhizal monitoring, trail maintenance
  • Land Partner: Offer foraging access on private land in exchange for ecological monitoring and stewardship
  • Master Forager / Mentor: Lead identification walks, teach workshops, review member observations, advise chapter leads
  • Citizen Scientist: Contribute to regional atlases, phenology tracking, rare species monitoring, DNA barcoding projects

No experience required — only curiosity, respect for the land, and willingness to learn.

Terms of Service

Last updated: June 2026

1. Foraging Safety

Foraging involves inherent risks. Always verify plant and fungi identifications with experienced foragers before consuming. Never eat a wild mushroom unless you are 100% certain of its identity.

2. Ethical Harvesting

Respect local habitats, harvest sustainably, take only what is abundant, and obtain required permits on public and private lands. Follow Leave No Trace principles.

3. Liability

QuantumAggForage and its chapter leads are not liable for injuries, misidentifications, or adverse reactions resulting from foraging activities. Participants assume all risks.

4. Community Conduct

Members are expected to treat each other, landowners, and the land with respect. Harassment, poaching, or destructive behavior will result in removal from the community.
